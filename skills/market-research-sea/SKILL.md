# Regional Market Research

A region is not a market. Treating one as a single market is the most common and most expensive research error: the numbers add up, the strategy does not survive the first country.

## Segment before sizing

Size per country, then decide whether to aggregate. A regional total is only meaningful when the go-to-market is genuinely shared, and it usually is not — regulation, payment rails, channel mix, and language differ per country even where the product does not.

Within each country, segment by language and urbanity before demographics. In much of the world these predict buying behaviour and channel reach better than age brackets do.

## What to establish per country

- **Regulatory position.** Licensing, advertising restrictions, data protection regime, and sector rules. Financial, health, education, and children's categories diverge sharply between neighbours. Establish this at research time; discovering it at launch invalidates the plan.
- **Channel reality.** Which platforms people actually use, and for what. Search-first and social-first markets need different content strategies. Messaging apps are a commerce channel in some markets and only a chat app in others.
- **Payment rails.** Card penetration, bank transfer, e-wallets, cash on delivery. Checkout design follows from this, and a checkout built for the wrong rail converts near zero.
- **Language and register.** Which language people search in, which they buy in, and which they complain in — these are often different. Code-switching is normal; write for how people actually speak, not for the official language.
- **Trust signals.** Local awards, certifications, religious or community endorsements, and marketplace presence carry weight that foreign proof points do not.

## Sources

Prefer, in order: official statistics offices and regulators; platform-published market data; reputable industry reports with a stated methodology; company disclosures. Treat aggregator blog posts as leads to a primary source, never as the source.

For every figure record: the number, its unit, its year, its geography, and its source. A market size without those five is unusable, and one repeated across three articles without a primary source is usually one estimate wearing three hats.

## Field checks that beat desk research

- Search the category in each target language and read what actually ranks.
- Read the negative reviews of the incumbents. That is where the unmet need is stated plainly.
- Price-check locally rather than converting from a home-market price. Willingness to pay does not track exchange rates.
- Talk to people who sell in the market. Distributors and agencies know the channel economics that no report contains.

## Rules

- Never present a converted currency figure without the rate and date used.
- Never extrapolate one country's conversion rate onto another in the region.
- Mark every projection as a projection, with its assumptions listed. An unlabelled forecast becomes a fact within two documents.
- When evidence exists for only one country, say the strategy is validated for that country. Regional claims need regional evidence.
