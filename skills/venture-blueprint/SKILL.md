# Venture Blueprint

Five stages, each with a written exit test. The point of the gates is to make *stopping* a normal outcome rather than a failure, so effort concentrates on ventures that have earned it.

Run one stage at a time. Do not let a strong idea skip validation because it is exciting.

## Stage 1 — Ideation

Produce: problem statement in the customer's words, the segment that has it worst, why now, and three to five distinct approaches.

**Exit test:** a named segment, a problem they already spend money or effort on, and at least one approach whose failure would be informative. An idea that cannot fail is not yet an idea.

## Stage 2 — Validation

Produce: evidence that the problem is real and the proposed solution is wanted. Interviews with people who are not friends. A concrete demand signal — pre-orders, a waitlist with a cost to join, a letter of intent, or a paid pilot.

**Exit test:** demand evidence that cost someone something. Sign-ups on a free form are not demand. Record the sample size and how the people were found, because selection bias is the usual reason a validated idea dies later.

Kill criteria belong here, written before the evidence is gathered.

## Stage 3 — Go-to-market

Produce: positioning, pricing and packaging, the first channel, the unit economics, and the first ninety days as a dated plan.

**Exit test:** one channel with a plausible, arithmetic-backed path to acquisition cost below expected gross margin per customer. One channel, done properly, beats four half-run experiments. State the assumptions and which are untested.

## Stage 4 — Growth

Produce: the loop that compounds — the mechanism by which each cohort makes the next cheaper or larger — plus retention data and a measurement plan.

**Exit test:** retention flattens rather than decaying to zero, and at least one loop shows a measurable contribution. Growth without retention is a leaking bucket with a bigger tap.

## Stage 5 — Options

Produce: the strategic choices now open — scale, focus a segment, license, partner, spin out, or wind down — each with what it costs and what it forecloses.

**Exit test:** a decision with a date and an owner. A venture permanently "exploring options" has already chosen drift.

## Cross-stage rules

- **Carry state forward explicitly.** Each stage writes a document the next stage reads. Later stages must not silently re-derive earlier conclusions; if they disagree with a prior stage, say so and revisit that stage.
- **Separate evidence from assumption** in every artefact. Mark each line as one or the other. Most venture failures are assumptions that got promoted to facts by repetition.
- **Regulated categories change the gate.** Financial, health, and children's products need the compliance pass at Stage 2, not at launch — the regulatory position can invalidate the model itself.
- **Record what was not tested.** The unexamined assumption that kills a venture is almost never a surprise in hindsight.
- Numbers carry their source and date. An unsourced market size is decoration.
